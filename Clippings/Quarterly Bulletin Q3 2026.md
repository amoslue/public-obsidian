For a small open economy such as Ireland, fiscal policy has a key role to play in delivering sustainable economic growth and low inflation over the medium term. To achieve this, the fiscal stance should be countercyclical, i.e. it should support the economy when it is operating below its sustainable potential (i.e. when unemployment is high) and conversely, it should reduce demand when the economy is operating above its sustainable potential and excessive inflationary risks are present. Current and projected economic and labour market conditions indicate that the economy does not require additional budgetary stimulus at present. A benefit of pursuing a countercyclical policy stance in the present economic conditions is that larger fiscal buffers could be accumulated, which could then be deployed when a downturn in economic activity occurs and unemployment rises.
