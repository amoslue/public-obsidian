From Acemoglu and Autor/Restrepo, we get how technology influences the wage:

$c$ is the rental price of a machine, $\gamma(x^*)$ is the “edge at the marginal human task” which is essentially how much better a human is than a machine at something which could be automated. $\gamma(x^*)$ you should think of as “technology”, and it can go up or down depending on what kind of technology is invented.

$a \cdot c$ is how much machines cost you need to make a machine $\lambda \cdot w$ is how much labor cost you need to make a machine, and $b \cdot r$ is how much land, oil, ore, other fixed stuff you need to make a machine.

housing prices and rents rising in relation to the other things we buy should not be surprising, the model predicts that if $\gamma(x^*)$ falls which kinda happened around the 1970s, and really got going after the internet took off.
